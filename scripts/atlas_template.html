Module 1 · Urban growth

The city added a third more built-up land in eight years

  • Built-up land rose from {{built0}} to {{built2}} km² between {{y0}} and {{y2}}. Growth runs east along the Jinja Road and north into Wakiso.
  • The core densified: sparse fabric turned dense across about 112 km².
  • Classifier agreement with its training labels is {{agree}} % (kappa 0.78–0.80) on held-out samples.

Before citing. Agreement with training labels is not map accuracy. 350 validation points still need to be labelled by eye. Tree-cover gains probably include seasonal cropland confusion.

Module 2 · Thermal classification

Compact neighbourhoods run 5 to 7 °C above the city

  • The hottest class covers {{hot2}} km² in {{y2}}. About 132 km² are hot in all three epochs: these are the persistent hotspots.
  • Compact mid- and low-rise zones are the hottest Local Climate Zones. Large low-rise sheds and warehouses warm fastest. Dense tree cover sits about 2 °C below the mean.
  • Surface temperature covers 100 % of the area, against about 60 % for the standard product, and agrees with it at r = {{r_min}}–{{r_max}}.
Module 3 · Informal settlements

The newest informal areas warmed fastest

  • Informal-type morphology sits +{{inf}} °C above the city land mean, about 1 °C hotter than formal dense fabric (+{{formal}} °C).
  • Cells that became informal-type between {{y0}} and {{y2}} warmed {{became}} °C relative to the city. Stable formal dense cells warmed {{formalchg}} °C.
  • Detected well: Kawempe Mbogo, Kazo Angola, Kifumbira, Kalerwe.

Before citing. The settlement layer is a morphology rule, not a survey boundary. It agrees with an independent footprint source on 42 % of its area, misses mixed-use areas such as Kisenyi and Kivulu, and its 2017–2025 growth is inflated by building-count noise.

Module 4 · Where to plant

{{vh_km2}} km² where heat, people and bare ground meet

  • Each extra 10 % of canopy goes with {{cool10}} °C cooler surfaces in built-up cells.
  • The very-high priority class holds about {{vh_people}},000 residents and almost no trees today (0.3 % canopy).
  • Planting half the open land in high and very-high cells adds {{newcanopy}} km² of canopy and cools those cells by about {{treecool}} °C.

Before citing. The cooling model explains about a third of the variation in surface temperature (R² 0.32). Surface cooling is larger than air-temperature cooling.

Module 0 · The cube

Sun overhead, breeze from the lake

  • At 0.3° N the noon sun never drops below 66°. Flat or 5–10° panels are best; tilt changes yield by under 1.5 %.
  • East and west walls take about 2.1 times the direct sun of north and south walls. Long axes should run east–west.
  • From 09:00 the Lake Victoria breeze builds from the south, peaking at 15:00 (74 % of hours from S or SE). Long south facades catch it; north–south gaps let it through.
Module 5 · Rooftop solar

Roof space is not the constraint

  • {{roofs}} million roofs could host about {{gwp}} GWp and generate {{twh}} TWh a year, at {{yield_}} kWh per kWp.
  • The largest tenth of roofs alone exceeds Uganda's 2024 distribution sales of 4.7 TWh (Umeme).
  • The real limits are daytime demand, grid absorption, storage and finance. That is the question for the solar chapter.
